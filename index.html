Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How do I calculate syringe units for peptide reconstitution?
To find the exact unit mark: divide your vial's total milligrams (e.g. 5 mg) by the volume of Bacteriostatic Water added in milliliters (e.g. 2 mL) to find concentration (2.5 mg/mL). Then divide your desired dose (e.g. 0.25 mg) by 2.5 mg/mL to find the volume needed (0.1 mL). On a standard U-100 syringe (where 100 units = 1.0 mL), 0.1 mL corresponds to exactly 10 units.
Why is Bacteriostatic Water required instead of plain sterile water?
Bacteriostatic Water contains 0.9% benzyl alcohol, an effective antimicrobial preservative. Under United States Pharmacopeia (USP) guidelines, multi-dose vials reconstituted with bacteriostatic water can be stored refrigerated (2°C–8°C) for up to 28–56 days. Sterile water lacks preservative and must be used immediately or discarded within 24 hours due to bacterial contamination risks.
What is the titration schedule for Semaglutide and Tirzepatide in clinical trials?
According to the STEP-1 trial (NEJM 2021) and FDA product labeling, Semaglutide begins at 0.25 mg once weekly for 4 weeks, titrating monthly to 0.5 mg, 1.0 mg, 1.7 mg, and 2.4 mg. For Tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-1, NEJM 2022), patients start at 2.5 mg weekly for 4 weeks, then escalate in 2.5 mg increments every 4 weeks to 5.0 mg, 7.5 mg, 10.0 mg, 12.5 mg, or 15.0 mg.
